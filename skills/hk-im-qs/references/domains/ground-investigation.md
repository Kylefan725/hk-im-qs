## 1. Establish the task and document basis

1. Read [project basis and evidence controls](../project-basis.md) first; use its project basis, evidence and per-row status controls.
2. Limit the package to specified instrument-related GI and its interfaces with monitoring or ground improvement. Identify unrelated building work as outside this reference's scope.
3. Record whether the task is a tender takeoff, rate build-up, measurement check, interim valuation, variation assessment or final account.
4. Establish the incorporated measurement method, particular preambles, specifications, drawings, addenda, instructions, contract family and pricing mechanism.
5. Read the [GI source guide](../ground-investigation-sources.md) and relevant entries in the [source register](../source-register.json).
6. Use `CEDD-SMM-1992` for measurement only if adopted; inspect the actual rule and any project amendment. Do not infer billing rules from technical specifications or instrument manufacturers' installation instructions.
7. Use `CEDD-GS-2020`, `GEO-GEOGUIDE-2` and `GEO-GEOGUIDE-3` at the evidence level recorded. Published amendments do not automatically amend the signed contract.

The verified specification route is GS 2020 Volume 1, Section 7, Part 2 for GI and Part 6 for geotechnical instrumentation. Follow the source guide to the relevant text; do not substitute section numbers from a different standard.

## 2. Collect project and field inputs

| Input | Record and reconcile |
|---|---|
| Investigation schedule | Hole/station IDs, coordinates, datum, purpose, diameter, method, planned depth and instructions |
| Instrument schedule | Instrument ID/type, hole link, sensor/tip depths, casing/tube/cable details and specification revision |
| Field records | Daily drilling logs, strata records, achieved depth, samples/tests, progress interruptions and signed instruction references |
| Installation evidence | As-built levels, filters/seals/grout intervals, acceptance checks, serial numbers, photographs and handover records |
| Observation evidence | Baseline, dates/times, reading rounds, quality flags, corrections, reports and requested monitoring period |
| Commercial documents | BQ/schedule item, preambles, included activities, quotations, rate basis, prior claimed and certified quantities |
| Site constraints | Access/possession, underground-service clearance, working windows, water access, protection, waste and reinstatement |

Record which data are planned, performed, checked, accepted or disputed. A field signature is evidence of the recorded work; do not treat it as agreement of payment entitlement without the contract basis.

## 3. Build linked registers before measuring

Maintain one traceable chain: location → hole → depth interval → sample/test/instrument → dated activity → bill item.

- Give holes, instruments, tests and reading rounds separate IDs; a hole containing more than one component is not multiple drilled holes.
- Record collar coordinates and level, depth zero, survey datum, inclination, measured depth and vertical depth as applicable. Do not silently interchange them.
- Record from/to depths with explicit units; distinguish drilled depth, recovered-core length, installed pipe/casing length and tip/screen interval.
- Check duplicate IDs, inconsistent revisions, reversed intervals, overlaps, gaps and instruments linked to nonexistent holes.
- Preserve original records and an adjustment log when correcting data. Keep superseded records out of current totals without losing their history.
- Link relocated, abandoned, redrilled and replacement work to the original ID and the instruction/reason; do not overwrite the original record.

## 4. Reconcile drilling and investigation activities

1. Reconcile the instructed/planned locations with the performed hole register; list additions, omissions and relocations.
2. Establish the actual measurement origin and endpoint for the bill item from the adopted rule; use field depths only after checking that basis.
3. Split intervals only where the contract requires distinct methods, sizes, materials, depth bands or other classifications. Keep the evidence for each boundary.
4. Do not transfer a geological description directly into a payable material category without reading the applicable definition.
5. Keep drilling advance, recovery, reaming, casing, obstructions and repeated drilling as separate activity records. Decide inclusion or separate measurement from the documents.
6. Identify mobilisations, moves, access works, standing time and water-based operations separately in the resource log; verify whether and how they are payable.
7. Reconcile cumulative depth with daily increments, repeated intervals and termination records; investigate differences before valuation.
8. For proposed work, preserve uncertain ground conditions and termination depths as explicit assumptions or provisional scope.

## 5. Reconcile specified sampling and testing

1. Include SPT, CPT/CPTu, permeability, pumping, geophysical or other testing only where specified or instructed and within this package's scope.
2. Link each test/sample to its location, depth/interval, date, equipment, instruction and report.
3. Distinguish a test event from its readings, stages, depths, repeats and report files. None automatically establishes the bill unit.
4. Record attempted, incomplete, invalid, repeated and accepted tests separately, including reason and instruction. Do not assume all attempts are payable or that every repeat is unpaid.
5. Reconcile collected specimens, dispatched specimens, laboratory receipts and reported results when laboratory work forms part of the instrument-related scope.
6. Check transport, containers, calibration, attendance, reporting and retention against the relevant item coverage before adding separate amounts.

## 6. Resolve the GI–instrumentation interface

| Activity | Required commercial decision |
|---|---|
| Hole formation | Which GI item owns the hole; whether reuse, enlargement or redrilling is included |
| Installation | Which item owns the installed instrument, casing/tube, filter, seal, grout and protective headworks |
| Acceptance / baseline | Required checks, accepted records and whether included in installation or another express item |
| Ongoing readings | Reading scope, period, frequency, quality requirements and any express separate payment provision |
| Readout / telemetry | Supply versus hire, shared devices, data logger, power, communications, data service and ownership |
| Protection / maintenance | Responsibility, duration, replacement basis and evidence of damage or failure |
| Completion | Retention, removal, abandonment, sealing, reinstatement and final records |

Inspect CEDD SMM Section 2, clauses 2.27–2.30 where adopted. Its installation coverage includes reading/recording, calibration and maintenance: do not automatically create additional paid monitoring items. Record any project provision that changes this allocation.

Do not equate open-hole water observations, standpipe water levels and piezometric readings without checking the instrument and reference basis. This is a records check, not interpretation of groundwater design conditions.

For inclinometers, keep casing installation, orientation/as-built data, acceptance checks and reading surveys distinct in the records. For piezometers, preserve each tip or sensor identity and depth. Confirm technical acceptance with the responsible specialist; do not invent acceptance limits.

## 7. Reconcile completion and rates

1. Record completed, retained-for-monitoring and abandoned holes separately. Confirm sealing, backfilling, grouting, headworks and reinstatement requirements from the project documents.
2. Reconcile grout/filter/material deliveries with usage records where required. A delivery quantity is not automatically the measured installed quantity.
3. Separate reusable rigs, probes, readouts and loggers from permanent or consumable components. Check contractual ownership, recovery and any residual-credit provisions before pricing.
4. Build rates from sourced labour, equipment utilisation, materials, tests, transport, reporting and applicable overhead assumptions; record HKD, quote date and quantity/productivity basis.
5. Allocate a shared readout or rig once across the stated workload; explain the allocation method. Do not charge a full purchase to every instrument without a supporting basis.
6. Reconcile included activities before adding provisional sums, attendance, reports or maintenance. Avoid duplicate allowances across the GI and I&M packages.
7. For variations, link scope change and instruction to the applicable valuation mechanism. Actual resource expenditure alone does not establish entitlement.

## 8. Produce a reviewable quantity and valuation pack

| Hole / instrument / event ID | Date / interval / datum | Activity and evidence | BQ item / adopted rule | Raw quantity | Billing adjustment / unit | Current / cumulative quantity | Status / action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Include separate schedules for:

- Drilling intervals, samples/tests, instrument installations and monitoring activities, reconciled through shared IDs.
- Scope allocation and item-coverage decisions, with exact project and standard references.
- Planned, performed, claimed and certified quantities, including previous/current/cumulative amounts.
- Rate evidence, provisional amounts, disputed items, and changes in scope or duration.
- Missing evidence and the specific decision it prevents; identify the responsible reviewer.

Use `CALCULATION_ONLY` for arithmetic pending billing evidence, `NEEDS_SOURCE` for affected unresolved items, and `READY_FOR_QS_REVIEW` only after source applicability, inputs, quantities, inclusions and rate basis are checked. Continue unaffected items; do not convert missing values to zero.

## 9. Example and final checks

**Illustrative records only:** BH-01 advances from 0 to 30 m; an instrument is installed in that hole and later read on several dates. Maintain the drilling interval, instrument installation and reading events as distinct records. Do not count another 30 m of hole formation simply because the instrument also has a 30 m installation record, or turn each reading into an extra payment item without the billing provisions.

- Reconcile depth origin, intervals, hole IDs, installation records and current drawing revisions.
- Check that no test, mobilisation, report, shared readout or reading activity is counted twice.
- Distinguish execution/technical acceptance from measurement, certification and contractual entitlement.
- List unverified termination, material-classification, rejected-test and replacement decisions.
- Keep technical interpretation and design decisions with the responsible geotechnical/instrumentation professional.
- Use SI and HKD; retain official English titles and clause references in Traditional Chinese outputs when requested.
